The work that turns a website into phone calls

Getting found,
and answering first

A website only earns its keep if people find it and get an answer quickly. Those are two different jobs, and neither of them is building the site.

Where the calls actually come from

When someone searches for what you do and the name of your town, the map results sit at the top of the page, above every website on it. A small number of businesses get listed there. Getting into that block is separate work from having a good site, and for a local business it is usually the work that makes the phone ring.

Then there is what happens next. Someone fills in your form at nine in the evening. If your first reply reaches them at eleven the following morning, they have already called two other people. Speed is most of the difference between an enquiry and a job, and it is the part almost nobody has set up.

Your Google Business Profile

This is the listing that feeds the map results. Most are half filled in, claimed by someone who has left the business, or quietly wrong about the hours.

  • Claimed and verified in your name, not a previous web company's
  • Categories and service areas set to what you actually do and where
  • Hours, holiday hours and contact details kept correct
  • Real photographs of your work, added regularly rather than once
  • Posts and answered questions, which keep the listing active
  • Your name, address and phone number matching everywhere they appear online

Reviews, without chasing anyone

Reviews decide both where you rank in that map block and who gets called out of the businesses listed in it. Asking for them by hand is the sort of job that gets done for two weeks and then forgotten.

  • An automatic request after a completed job, sent while it is still fresh
  • A link that opens straight to the review box, so nobody has to go hunting
  • New reviews flagged to you, and replies drafted so every one gets answered
  • A poor review seen the day it lands, not a month later

Answering first

The follow-up runs whether or not you are free to run it, which is the whole point of it.

  • A form submission or a missed call gets a reply within about a minute
  • A short follow-up sequence if they go quiet, which stops the moment they answer
  • Every enquiry in one list with its status, instead of three inboxes and a notepad
  • Booking and reminder messages, which is what brings no-shows down

What to expect, and when

The follow-up work pays off immediately, because a reply that used to take a morning now takes a minute. The ranking work is slower, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something:

WhenWhat should be happening
First month Profile claimed, corrected and verified. Instant replies and review requests live, so no enquiry sits overnight. Position in the map results has usually not moved yet.
Months two and three Reviews arriving steadily rather than occasionally. The listing is active, and the report starts showing how many people found you through the map rather than by name.
Month four onwards This is where it compounds: more reviews and a consistent listing feed the ranking, and the ranking brings the calls that produce more reviews.

Nobody can promise you a position — Google decides that, and any agency guaranteeing first place is guessing. What I can promise is that the work is done, and that the monthly report shows you exactly what moved and what did not.

Verification is the one step neither of us controls. Google sometimes wants to post a card to your address before it will hand the listing over, and that can take a week or two. Everything else moves as quickly as we do.
