Why it is worth having a website

The customers you never
hear from

Most businesses that are losing work online never find out. The people who looked you up and could not find you don't call to tell you.

What happens before someone calls you

Almost nobody picks a local business cold any more. They search for it, or they get a recommendation and then search for the name to check it out. That search is the moment you either exist or you don't — and it usually happens on a phone, often in the evening, long after anyone is answering yours.

If nothing comes up, or what comes up is a listing with an old phone number and no photos, the next result gets the job. That customer was already sold. You just weren't there to take it.

What a website does that nothing else does

It works while you don't

A site answers questions, shows your work and takes an enquiry at nine at night, on a Sunday, and while you are on a job with your phone in the truck.

You own it

A social page belongs to the platform. It can change its rules, bury your posts or disappear. Your site, your domain and your customer list stay yours.

It decides how you look

People judge a business by the first thing they see. A clean, current site says you are established and take the work seriously — before you have said a word.

It is what search can find

Ranking for "what you do" plus "your city" needs somewhere to send people. Without a site there is nothing to rank.

What makes one worth having

Plenty of businesses have a website and still lose the same work, because the site was built to be looked at rather than used. The difference is short:

  • It works on a phone. That is where most of your visitors are. A site that needs pinching and zooming loses them in seconds.
  • It loads fast. People leave slow pages, and search engines rank them lower for it.
  • There is an obvious way to reach you. A visible phone number and a form that works, not a contact page that goes nowhere.
  • It says what you do and where. Vague copy is why a site never shows up for the searches that matter.
  • It is current. Old prices, closed hours and a copyright date from four years ago all read as "probably out of business".
